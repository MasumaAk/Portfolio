My story

How my experience shaped my work.

“I tend to start with the decision that needs to be made, then work backward to the data and analysis that can help answer it.”How I approach analytics

I started on the business side. My early career in Bangladesh put me close to customers, campaigns, enterprise technology projects, proposals, tenders, contracts, and commercial decisions. Over time, data became a bigger part of how I worked through performance reporting, CRM information, customer trends, cost analysis, and KPI dashboards.

I moved to the USA to deepen that work through analytics. My M.S. in Business Analytics at Kent State University strengthened my technical foundation in SQL, Python, visualization, databases, statistical modeling, and machine learning. At Pipeline Packaging, I then applied analytics directly to sales, pricing, margin, and data validation work.

Today, I bring the business and analytical sides together. My current work includes commercial analytics, large-scale outcomes research, and mobile application security analysis. Across these different settings, I am interested in whether the data and analysis are reliable enough to support the decision being made.

Independent project • synthetic dataCommercial analytics • SQL • Python • Power BI

EnterpriseIQ

Built an independent commercial intelligence prototype using synthetically generated B2B distributor data: 300 accounts, 150 products, 25 sales representatives, and 100,000 records each for orders, fulfillment, and invoices. The connected tables enable testing pricing leakage, profitability, fulfillment, and account risk. This is simulated data, not actual company performance. The decision question is where commercial teams should investigate leakage and service risks first.

Illustrative synthetic-data results: 27.9% leakage for a flagged representative group versus 4.2% baseline. These figures are not real-world business outcomes.

ResearchLarge-scale research • Python • GLMs

ADRD Outcomes Research

Contributing to an ongoing collaborative research study using a seven-year national inpatient dataset of approximately 22.6 million records, including about 2.3 million ADRD hospitalizations. My work includes statistical analysis of mortality, length of stay, and hospital costs using logistic, negative binomial, and gamma regression models. The research examines how fall timing and other patient factors relate to these outcomes. Manuscript in preparation; not yet published.

Research • evolving studySecurity research • Python

Mobile Application Security Research

At Kent State's SPIRIT Lab, I build Python workflows with PyMuPDF and pandas to extract and analyze mobile application security reports. In an earlier phase, I analyzed reports for six apps: Alibaba, TikTok, LinkedIn, Uber, Airbnb, and Strava, comparing manifest settings, code findings, network security severity, and trackers. The research asks which security indicators are meaningful and reliable enough to support comparisons across apps. The same focus on data quality and trustworthy measurement that guides my commercial analytics work also shapes this research.

Academic / portfolio projectClassification • scikit-learn

Bank Customer Churn

Compared Logistic Regression and Random Forest on 10,000 customers. The Random Forest reached 84.3% accuracy, 66.3% recall, and 0.855 ROC-AUC, showing why accuracy alone was insufficient for the business question. The decision question is which customers need retention attention, not merely which model has the highest overall accuracy.
